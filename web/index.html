Optical Workbench is a 3D planner for laser spectroscopy setups. Lay out a breadboard with real lasers, mirrors and detectors, trace the beam through every bounce, and export the parts list, all in your browser.

Step 1 of 4

Choose a breadboard

Start from a Thorlabs MB1218, a metric 600 × 450 mm board, a model you import, or your own dimensions. Add a second board beside the first when the setup outgrows it.

  • 25 mm and 1 inch hole grids
  • Custom size, thickness, pitch and margins
  • Snap parts to the holes as you place them
Step 2 of 4

Place real parts

Pick lasers, mirror mounts, detectors and post holders from the library, then click a hole to drop them in. Move, rotate about the mounting point, set heights and stack parts in holders.

  • Supplied QCL and NanoPlus lasers, flat and parabolic mirrors, a VIGO detector
  • Import your own STEP models, converted on your computer
  • Group parts and save them as reusable assemblies
Step 3 of 4

Trace the beam

Click the laser, each optic it bounces off, and the detector. The beam follows its components when you move them, so you can try a new arrangement without redrawing it.

  • Several beams per setup, each with its own colour
  • Beam diameter, opacity and expansion along the path
  • Warnings when a bounce no longer lines up
Step 4 of 4

Check the layout and export

Look down from above, measure between optical centres, and compare heights against a reference plane. When it looks right, export a build sheet and an Excel parts list for ordering.

  • Top, front, side and 3D views
  • Two-point and optical-centre measurements
  • Parts list with Thorlabs part numbers

Everything a bench layout needs

The tools you reach for when planning an optical setup, built around real hardware and real hole grids.

Beams that behave like beams

Draw a beam from the laser through each optic to the detector. Points stay attached to their parts, so the path updates as you rearrange the bench.

  • Several beams per setup, named and coloured
  • Diameter, opacity and divergence along the path
  • Advanced ray preview for quick geometric checks

Snaps to the hole grid

Parts land on real 25 mm or 1 inch holes, with exact positions from the board centre.

Real hardware library

Import your own STEP

Bring in any STEP model. It's converted on your computer and never uploaded. Set its mounting anchors and optical centre once, then reuse it.

Mount, stack and group

Put parts in adjustable post holders, set heights above the board, rotate about the mounting point, and save groups as assemblies.

Measure and check

Measure between any two points or between optical centres, show optical-centre markers, and compare heights against a beam-height reference plane.

Export for the lab

Generate a build sheet and an Excel parts list with part numbers, and save the whole layout as an .optics.json file to share or keep.
